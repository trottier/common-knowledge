# Water draft source review

The English and Spanish water drafts now include sourced alternative-water
precautions, boiling steps and the existing EPA bleach table. Both retain their
original draft/machine status and named human medical review gate. Spanish's
English-body hash was refreshed after translating the changes; this records
freshness, not human approval.

The checked [EPA English page](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/emergency-disinfection-drinking-water)
and [official Spanish page](https://espanol.epa.gov/espanol/desinfeccion-de-agua-potable-en-situaciones-de-emergencia)
specify one minute at a rolling boil and three above 5,000 feet. Both pages also
pair 5,000 feet with 1,000 meters, which are not equivalent. The draft uses only
the published feet threshold; it does not repeat that metric parenthesis.
The [CDC advisory page](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/drinking-water-advisories-an-overview.html)
uses 6,500 feet instead. The draft explicitly retains this disagreement and the
unknown-elevation branch as a human water-safety release-review item.

The [CDC alternative-source page](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/how-to-find-clean-water-in-an-emergency.html)
supports safely made ice, canned produce liquid and excluding pool/spa water
from drinking. The advisory page separately excludes water and ice from plumbed
refrigerators during an advisory. Safe extraction from pipes and tap-water
heaters, with energy isolation, scalding and contamination precautions, is still
an explicit gap. An untested valve procedure has not been substituted.

The heat-illness draft also distinguishes heat stroke from heat exhaustion,
using the [CDC NIOSH first-aid guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/heat-stress/about/illnesses.html).
Heavy sweating does not exclude heat stroke. Both languages retain clinical
review for cooling, swallowing precautions and absent emergency response.
The existing CPR numbers now also cite the specific MedlinePlus adult article,
rather than relying only on its general CPR index. No protocol is marked reviewed.
