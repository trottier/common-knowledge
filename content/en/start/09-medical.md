## Severe bleeding

Call 911. Press firmly on an external wound with a clean cloth.
Keep pressure on it.
If blood soaks through, add cloth without taking the first cloth away.
Do not press on an injured eye or pull out an embedded object. [@bleeding]

**If 911 does not answer:** keep pressure on the wound. Ask someone else to seek help.
[[VERIFY: clinician must approve the no-response branch and add a reviewed tourniquet protocol for uncontrolled limb bleeding.]]

## Hands-only CPR for a teen or adult

If the person does not respond and is not breathing normally, call 911 and get an AED.
Put both hands in the center of the chest.
Push hard at 100–120 compressions per minute, about 2 inches deep.
Let the chest rise fully between pushes. Follow the AED's voice prompts. [@cpr]

Do not use this adult-only summary for a child or a drowning emergency.
[[VERIFY: clinician must supply those breathing-rescue protocols and exact CPR stop conditions.]]

**If 911 does not answer:** continue CPR while another person tries to find help and an AED. [@cpr]
[[VERIFY: clinician must approve the no-response branch.]]

## Recovery position

If a person is unresponsive but breathing normally, call 911.
When you do not suspect a spine injury, turn them gently onto their side.
Keep watching their breathing.
Do not give food or drink to an unresponsive person. [@recovery]

**If 911 does not answer:** stay with them and watch their breathing.
[[VERIFY: clinician must approve spine-injury precautions, airway positioning and the no-response branch.]]

## Burns

For a small heat burn, cool the skin with cool water.
Do not use ice or break blisters.
Seek care for a large or deep burn or a burn on the face, hands or genitals. [@burns]

**If 911 does not answer:** protect the area and keep seeking medical help.
[[VERIFY: clinician must approve cooling duration, dressings, chemical and electrical burns, and the no-response branch.]]

## Heat illness

Confusion or loss of consciousness in the heat can mean heat stroke.
Call 911 and begin cooling the person.
Move them out of the heat, remove outer clothing and wet their skin.
Do not give a drink to someone who cannot safely swallow. [@heat_illness]

**If 911 does not answer:** keep cooling and ask someone else to seek help.
[[VERIFY: clinician must approve cooling methods, heat-exhaustion care and the no-response branch.]]

## Hypothermia

Call 911 for suspected hypothermia.
Move the person gently out of the cold.
Replace wet clothes with dry layers and blankets.
Do not use a heating pad, hot water or alcohol. [@hypothermia]

**If 911 does not answer:** stay with them and protect them from more heat loss.
[[VERIFY: clinician must approve warming, handling, breathing checks and the no-response branch.]]

## Oral rehydration

For diarrhea-related fluid loss, use packaged oral rehydration salts with the exact amount of safe water stated on the packet.
Do not guess the water amount or add extra salt. [@oral_rehydration]

**If 911 does not answer:** seek a health worker for severe dehydration.
[[VERIFY: clinician must approve signs of severe dehydration, small-sip guidance, infant care, a measured homemade recipe when no packets exist, and the no-response branch.]]

## Medication continuity

Keep the medicine label with the medicine.
Check its storage directions after a power outage.
Ask a pharmacist, clinician or manufacturer about a medicine exposed to heat or floodwater. [@fda_medicines]
Do not guess a replacement dose. [@project_spec]

**If 911 does not answer:** seek a pharmacist or clinician and keep the label available.
[[VERIFY: clinician must approve life-sustaining medication decisions, emergency refill guidance and the no-response branch.]]
