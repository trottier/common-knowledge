## Expect delays

A computer attack can disrupt health-care and billing systems across the country. [@hhs_cyber_disruption]
Hospital care can be delayed. [@hhs_cyber_patientcare]
911 centers can lose computer services or switch to manual work. [@cisa_911_disruptions]

## Use what you have

Keep some cash in small bills if you have it. [@cdc_emergency_paperwork]
Do not delay urgent care while looking for cash or papers. [@project_spec]

Keep paper copies of key identity, insurance, and care documents. [@cdc_emergency_paperwork]
Write down your medicines, label strengths, usual doses, and allergies. [@fda_medicines]
Copy the label or your care plan; do not guess a missing dose. [@project_spec]
Keep these papers private; do not post them on a shared channel. [@project_spec]

## Ask about medicine

Ask a pharmacist what to do if the prescription system is down. [@fda_medicines]
Ask about emergency refills; do not assume a refill is allowed. [@fda_medicines]
[[VERIFY: state emergency-refill rules, cited per state.]]

## If help is delayed

Allow for slower care while staff work without their usual systems. [@hhs_cyber_patientcare] [@cisa_911_disruptions]
[[VERIFY: confirm current hospital paper-workflow guidance and clinical review of this outage section.]]
Plan to support your household while services recover. [@project_spec]

If 911 does not answer, use **Medical basics** and seek nearby qualified help when safe.
Do not enter a dangerous area to find help. [@project_spec]
