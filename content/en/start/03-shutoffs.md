## Gas

- If you smell gas or hear gas leaking, get everyone outside right away.
- Do not use a light switch, phone or other electrical device before leaving.
- Call the gas company from outside the home. [@cpsc_post_storm]

- The gas tag is a label, not an instruction to shut off every meter.
- Do not practice by turning the gas off.
- Gas meters have different shutoff procedures.
- If the gas is off, leave it off.
- Only a qualified professional may turn it back on. [@fema_utilities]

[[VERIFY: utility safety reviewer must approve the outside-valve procedure, safe access and valve identification. Do not publish a universal quarter-turn instruction for every gas installation.]]

## Water main

- Find where the main water pipe enters the home.
- Use the household main valve, not the street valve in the curb box.
- Closing the household main can keep stored water from draining out.
- Ready.gov advises closing it if cracked pipes may pollute the home's supply. [@fema_utilities]

[[VERIFY: utility reviewer must approve safe valve identification and operation, including when a valve is unfamiliar, damaged or stuck. Add the approved procedure before release.]]

## Main breaker

- If you suspect a gas leak, leave before touching any electrical control. [@cpsc_post_storm]
- Do not check the electrical system if you are wet, standing in water or unsure it is safe. [@fema_ready_guide]

- If it is safe to use the circuit panel, turn off the individual circuits first.
- Then turn off the main circuit. [@fema_utilities]
- Have an electrician check flood-damaged wiring before power is restored. [@fema_ready_guide]

[[VERIFY: electrician must approve panel identification, when not to approach or operate it, and solar/battery/generator hazards. Switching off the main is not permission to open a panel or work on wiring.]]

## Generator carbon monoxide

- Put a portable generator outdoors, at least 20 feet from doors, windows and vents.
- Never run it in a home or garage, even with doors open. [@cdc_generator] [@cdc_co_basics]
- Do not use it in a basement, shed or crawlspace.
- Point the exhaust away from nearby buildings. [@cpsc_generator_alert]

- Keep the generator dry.
- Follow its instructions for wet weather; they may require waiting until rain or snow passes. [@cpsc_post_storm]
- Dry your hands before touching it. [@cpsc_generator_alert]

- Use outdoor-rated, heavy-duty extension cords with all three plug prongs.
- Check for cuts or tears.
- The cord's wattage rating must exceed the total load connected to it.
- Do not pinch or crush the cord in a door or window.
- Never plug a generator into a wall outlet to power house wiring. [@cpsc_generator_alert]

- Turn the generator off before adding fuel.
- Let it cool first.
- Do not store generator fuel inside the home or near a fuel-burning appliance. [@cpsc_generator_alert]

- Use a battery-powered or battery-backup CO alarm indoors. [@cdc_co_basics]
- If an alarm sounds, go outside immediately.
- Call 911 from outside. [@cpsc_post_storm]
- If you feel sick, dizzy or weak while using a generator, get to fresh air right away. [@cpsc_generator_alert]

[[VERIFY: named safety and clinical reviewers must approve generator placement, electrical connection, fuel handling and the poisoning-response wording.]]

## If emergency help does not answer

Stay out of the hazard area.
Do not go back inside a suspected gas or CO hazard to retry the call. [@cpsc_post_storm]
[[VERIFY: utility, rescue and clinical reviewers must approve no-response actions and re-entry restrictions; do not improvise gas or electrical repairs or attempt an unprotected rescue.]]
